02

A reconciliation method, not a headline promise.

  1. Remove the exclusions

    Eligible rake = total rake − ineligible rake. Both amounts must cover the same period and use the same unit.

  2. Apply the rate, then the cap

    Raw credit = eligible rake × rate / 100. Credited amount is the smaller of raw credit and the cap, if a cap is supplied.

  3. Subtract fees and use the full denominator

    Net credit = credited amount − fee. Effective rate = net credit / total rake × 100. Net rake cost = total rake − net credit.

30 → 14%

Follow the five lines of the calculation.

In a fictional statement, 1,000 units of rake include 200 ineligible units. The remaining 800 produce 240 at a 30% rate. A 150-unit cap reduces the credit to 150. After a 10-unit fee, the net is 140: 14% of all rake paid.

(1,000 − 200) × 0.30 = 240
min(240, 150) − 10 = 140
140 / 1,000 × 100 = 14%
03

The details that change the result.

Before entering a statement

  • Use rake actually allocated to you by the statement’s method, not total pot sizes.
  • Match the calculation period to the period used for the cap.
  • Only convert reward points using a documented conversion value; the tool does not infer one.
  • Keep units and any tax treatment consistent across the ledger.

Two rates answer different questions

The headline rate applies to eligible rake. The effective rate measures net credit against all rake paid. Neither rate alone establishes whether play is profitable: table results and other costs are outside this model.

For tiers, multiple currencies or conditional rewards, reconcile each tranche separately. Do not add percentages calculated from different bases. A higher advertised rate can still produce a lower net credit.

04

Common questions

Does 30% rake back always mean 30% of all rake?

No. Eligibility, credit caps and fees can reduce the effective return. The calculator keeps total rake as the denominator so the headline rate and the effective rate are easy to distinguish.

What does an empty cap mean?

It means no cap. A cap of zero is different: it limits credited rake back to zero before subtracting the fixed fee.

Can the net result be negative?

Yes. If fees exceed the credit, the net amount is negative. The tool preserves that result instead of hiding the cost by clamping it to zero.

What happens when total rake is zero?

Excluded rake must also be zero. Since an effective percentage cannot be divided by zero, the rate is shown as not applicable. A fee can still create a negative net amount.

Does this compare current poker room offers?

No. There are no live offers or room rankings. Allocation methods, contribution rules and point conversions vary; use the rules attached to your own statement.

Are my figures stored?

No. The form is processed locally with no account or upload. The result checks arithmetic; it does not establish that a provider owes or will pay a particular amount.
